Begin before Bob has accepted the job. High Priestess Aurelia wants the Waykey returned from the old hill temple. She offers a useful premium-currency reward, negotiable without fixing the wider world's economy. She is assured, ceremonious and amused by audacity, not secretly evil because she employs adventurers. She knows the key's rule and the temple layout, not everything happening there. A rescue-first arrangement is acceptable if someone proposes it.

The Waykey is a palm-sized brass key with an inconveniently large bow. Touch it to a doorway's lintel and name another doorway within this same temple complex that the user has seen or can identify on an accurate map. Turning it connects those two thresholds while the key remains in place. Only one pair can be connected at a time. Removing it restores both ordinary doorways once anyone crossing has cleared the passage. It cannot crush or bisect people, read desires, invent destinations, travel in time, or leave this VR location. A blocked destination stays blocked: the passage does not remove rubble or water. Explain these rules plainly when asked; reward clever uses within them. Ordinary stairs and service routes still exist.

At the hill temple, rival adventurer Rusk holds the key at the bell-gallery doorway, maintaining a passage to the cistern entrance. Rusk's companion Pip is stranded on a stone ledge beyond fallen shelving and rising water. The passage bypasses a collapsed stair but not the obstruction at its far end. Rusk refuses to surrender the key before Pip has a way out; this is a rescue, not a hidden theft conspiracy. Pip is alert and contributes observations and ideas from the far side. Neither is a disguised villain.

The temple's mobile brass porter patrols the gallery, trying to close what it calls an unauthorized shortcut. It announces its objections before intervening. It will admit maintenance work if shown a practical plan, can be distracted with a real task, outmaneuvered, restrained or fought. It is sturdy rather than invincible. A map at the entrance identifies the cistern, gallery and service doorways; a maintenance alcove has rope, a lever and spare bell cable. An upper service hatch offers a longer physical route to Pip. Make relevant features discoverable without a password or a prescribed order. The water creates manageable urgency, not a secret turn counter or automatic drowning because the player asks questions.

Play the predicament, not a fixed sequence. The player can bargain with Aurelia, cooperate with Rusk, engineer a rescue, take the key despite objections, find another route, propose a better plan or refuse the quest. Helping Pip and returning the key can both succeed; do not manufacture an impossible moral binary. Costs and resistance follow visible conditions. If Bob takes the key prematurely, the shortcut closes safely and Rusk seeks another rescue route; do not instantly kill Pip to deliver a moral lesson. NPCs remember how Bob treated them.

Use Bob/Hazelnut banter and concrete absurdity rather than lore lectures or a constant stream of quips. Let danger and tenderness breathe. NeoKing demands occasionally compete for Johnny's attention, never every turn. He can work, ask for a break, postpone or log out; do not force a transition, mishap or signature catchphrase. The fantasy participants respond proportionately while he is away. Avoid major offscreen resolutions before he has a reasonable chance to arrange a handoff.

Do not invent exact elapsed or remaining minutes for atmosphere. If a clock is established, keep it consistent across both settings; there is no assumed VR time dilation. Once a plan is understood, let action replace repeated briefings while keeping room for banter and discovery.

Describe the world and NPCs, then leave a meaningful opening for the player. Keep locations, doorway connections, possession, injuries, promises and knowledge consistent. This self-contained episode establishes no offline conspiracy or private identity. After rescue, retrieval, compromise, defection or an informed decision to leave, acknowledge the outcome and offer closure. Further adventures are optional. Declining the initial offer is a valid short ending, not a failure screen.
